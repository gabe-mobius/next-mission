# Military transition master checklist (starter)

This file is a general starter checklist for U.S. service members preparing to separate or retire from active duty. It is organized by how far you are from your separation or retirement date. It is not legal advice, and it does not replace your branch's transition office, TAP counselor, transportation office, or VA. Individual branch rules vary and change, so confirm each item for your own situation.

**Last full check: 2026-10-08**

Each row shows where a rule came from. `sourced` means the quote was copied from the linked official page on the date shown. `UNVERIFIED` means we could not confirm it on an allowed official site; treat it as a lead to check, not a fact. Run `python3 check_checklist.py` to confirm every sourced row has a valid source.

## Questions to ask first

Answer these before you use the checklist. They decide which rows apply to you.

- **Branch:** Which branch are you in (Army, Navy, Marine Corps, Air Force, Space Force, or Coast Guard)? Retirement application windows and SkillBridge limits differ by branch. Rows marked with a branch apply only to that branch; rows marked `all` apply to everyone.
- **Unit (optional):** Which unit or installation are you assigned to? Your local TAP office, transportation office, and SkillBridge coordinator are tied to it.
- **Retiring or separating:** Are you retiring (20 or more years, or a medical retirement) or separating before retirement? Each row's 'Applies to' column says `retiring`, `separating`, or `both`.
- **Planned date:** What is your planned separation or retirement date? The 'Due' column counts from that date (for example, `-365` means 1 year before, `+180` means 180 days after).
- **Spouse and/or children:** Do you have a spouse or children who might use your GI Bill or be covered by the Survivor Benefit Plan? If yes, read the '4 or more years out' section now. Transferring GI Bill benefits requires 4 more years of service.
- **Plans after:** Are you planning to work, go to school, or start a business? This decides which TAP track, SkillBridge, and perks matter most.
- **State:** Which state do you plan to live in? This affects your final move, TRICARE options (TRICARE Prime is only in some areas), state veteran benefits, and your job search.

## How to read the tables

- **Applies to:** `retiring`, `separating`, or `both`.
- **Branch:** `all`, or the branches the row applies to (`army`, `navy`, `marine-corps`, `air-force`, `space-force`, `coast-guard`).
- **Due:** time relative to your separation or retirement date. `-365` means 1 year before; `+180` means 180 days after; `0` means the date itself; `-180..-90` is a window from 180 to 90 days before; `none` means there is no fixed deadline. A value that is a whole multiple of 365 (for example `-365`, `-1460`, or `+1095`) means that many calendar years, so `-1460` from 2027-12-08 is 2023-12-08. Any other value is counted in days; 6 months is written as 183 days and 18 months as 548 days.
- **Window (perks only):** when you can claim a perk, counted the same way from your separation or retirement date. `-365..+365` means from 1 year before until 1 year after; `any..+365` means any time until 1 year after; `any` means there is no time limit stated on the source. `deadlines.py --perks` uses it.
- **ID:** a short, stable name for the row. The Change log uses it to record old and new values.
- When a row has more than one source, the URLs and quotes are numbered (1), (2), and so on in the same order.
- `deadlines.py` uses these columns to build a personal list from a profile file (see profile.example.json).

### How to record changes

From 2026-10-08 on, every change to a row's rule, deadline, number, due date, tags, or status is recorded in the 'Value changes' table below as **old value -> new value**, so a later check can spot contradictions.

- **Row ID** is the row's ID from the checklist tables. IDs do not change once published.
- **Field** is one of: `Rule / deadline / number`, `Due`, `Applies to`, `Branch`, `Status`, `Window` (perks).
- **Old value** is the exact cell text before the change. Use `(new row)` for a new row.
- **New value** is the exact cell text after the change. Use `(removed)` when a row is deleted.
- Add new entries at the bottom, oldest first.
- `check_checklist.py` fails if the newest New value for a Row ID and Field does not match that row's current cell (an unlogged or contradicting edit), if an entry's Old value does not match the previous New value for the same Row ID and Field, if a removed row still exists, or if a date is invalid.
