# State Veteran Benefits (Texas, Oklahoma, and how to add more states)

> **This is general information, not legal or tax advice. State rules change often. Before you make a decision, confirm the details with the state veterans agency (contacts are in each state's table) or the agency named in the source.**

Last full review: 2026-10-08 (America/Chicago).

## How to read this file

- Each state has one table. Each row is one benefit.
- **Status `sourced`** means we fetched the official page listed in "Source URL" on the date shown and confirmed that the words in "Source quote or value" appear on that page word for word. When a row quotes two passages from the same page, they are separated by `[...]`.
- **Status `UNVERIFIED`** means we could not confirm the point on an official state government page or on va.gov. Do not rely on an UNVERIFIED row without checking it yourself.
- Only official sources are used: state government domains (such as texas.gov and oklahoma.gov and their subdomains), `*.state.XX.us` domains, va.gov, and a short list of other official state agency domains that are named and explained in `check_state_benefits.py`. No blogs, news sites, law firms, or commercial "benefit guide" sites.
- `check_state_benefits.py` checks every `sourced` row for an allowed URL, a quote, and a valid date. Run `python3 check_state_benefits.py` after any edit.

A note on two non-government sites that were considered:
- **hhloans.com** is not used and is not allowed.
- **collegeforalltexans.com** was checked. It was run by the Texas Higher Education Coordinating Board (THECB), but it is not on a .gov domain, it refused our automated fetch (HTTP 403) on 2026-10-08, and THECB says its content has moved to its "My Texas Future" site. We therefore did not use it. Texas education rows cite the Texas Veterans Commission (tvc.texas.gov) instead. THECB's own official site is highered.texas.gov.

## How to add another state

Use the same method for any state a user names. Do the research yourself on official pages; do not copy from benefit roundup sites.

1. **Find the state veterans agency.** Use the VA's directory of state veterans affairs offices (https://department.va.gov/about/state-departments-of-veterans-affairs-office-locations/) to find the official agency and its website.
2. **Find the official agency for each topic.** Usually: the state revenue or tax department (income tax on retired pay); the state comptroller, revenue department, or county assessor guidance (property tax); the higher education board and the veterans agency (education); the state personnel or HR office and the state code (hiring preference); the motor vehicle or driver license agency (license designations, plates, registration); the veterans agency (veterans homes, state cemeteries); and the wildlife or parks department (hunting, fishing, parks).
3. **Use only allowed domains.** Allowed: the state's own government domains (for example `texas.gov`, `oklahoma.gov`, and their subdomains), any `*.state.XX.us` domain, and `va.gov`. If an official state agency uses a different domain (for example a `.com` or `.org`), you may use it only after you confirm it is the agency's own site, ideally because a page on the state's .gov domain links to it or names it.
